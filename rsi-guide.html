How to Trade Overbought & Oversold Zones with RSI (14)

Published by Fareed Market Research Team • 5 Min Read

Developed by J. Welles Wilder Jr. in 1978, the Relative Strength Index (RSI) remains one of the most widely used momentum oscillators in technical analysis. Measured on a scale from 0 to 100 using a standard 14-period lookback, RSI evaluates the velocity and magnitude of recent price changes to determine whether an asset is overvalued or undervalued in the short term.

Understanding Key RSI Thresholds

In standard market conditions, traders monitor three critical zones on the RSI scale:

  • Overbought Zone (Above 70): Indicates that buying pressure has pushed prices rapidly upward and an exhaustion pullback or consolidation may be imminent.
  • Oversold Zone (Below 30): Indicates heavy selling pressure that may have pushed the asset below fair short-term value, creating potential for a relief bounce.
  • The 50 Centerline: Acts as a trend divider. Readings consistently holding above 50 confirm bullish market structure, while readings below 50 confirm bearish dominance.

Combining RSI with EMA Crossovers

A common mistake among beginners is automatically selling every time RSI crosses above 70. During strong macro uptrends, RSI can remain overbought for extended periods while price continues climbing. Instead, experienced traders use RSI as a confirmation filter alongside moving averages:

For example, when the EMA 9 crosses above the EMA 20 and the RSI simultaneously crosses above the 50 level without yet reaching extreme 75+ territory, it signals fresh momentum with room to run. Conversely, if RSI drops below 30 right into a major support level, short-term traders look for bullish reversal candles to time a bounce.
